# R6 — Afya Siku (period care)

Feeds section 3.5 (package page). Prices checked 10 Nov 2026. Data file: `data/boxes/afya-siku.json` (built from this research; `final: false`, reasons in section 7).

## In plain language

- **Who:** girls and women who menstruate, from the first period to menopause. One pack a month, arriving a few days before her next period is due. She records her period dates in the app and the delivery date follows.
- **In the box every month (TSh 8,095 shelf value):** 18 day pads, 7 night or heavy-flow pads, 10 liners, a 100 g bar of mild soap for external washing and 6 small disposal bags. The counts are for a regular flow; she sets light, regular or heavy in the app.
- **First box (TSh 23,095):** the same goods plus a hot water bottle with a cover (TSh 15,000), and a small Afya-printed card on the cycle, disposal and clinic signs (not sold, no price).
- **Reusable choice:** a menstrual cup or a set of washable pads can replace most disposables. It is optional, ships once when chosen, and is priced at shelf (about TSh 40,000–44,000; a Tanzanian cup is about 20,000, estimate).
- **Medicines:** pain tablets (paracetamol or ibuprofen) and iron are listed but not sold or priced by Afya. The partner pharmacy sells them after the pharmacist checks what suits her (B4).
- **On its own, a monthly delivery loses money.** Goods worth TSh 8,095 cannot carry TSh 7,000 of packing and delivery. The 12-month plan would lose TSh 5,889 a box. Even a 3-month supply in one delivery loses TSh 5,662, and a 6-month supply still loses 2,825. A standalone delivery needs about TSh 56,900 of goods to break even, which is about 7 months of pads.
- **The fix chosen: it travels inside another Afya box.** The pack goes into the household's other Afya box (for example Afya Glow or Mama na Mtoto) on that box's delivery, timed before her period. A member with no other box collects it in store. With no box and no delivery of its own, it earns TSh 1,606 a month on the 1-month plan and 1,111 on the 6- and 12-month plans (on planning landed costs).
- **Member price, every month:** TSh 8,000 (1 month), 7,500 (6 months), 7,500 (12 months). On a box this small, rounding to the nearest 500 makes the 6- and 12-month prices equal. **First box:** 22,500 / 21,500 / 21,000.
- **Taste of luxury** (priced as its own line): Premium adds Cadbury Bournville dark chocolate 80 g (TSh 4,000). Luxury adds Lindt Excellence 85% dark chocolate 100 g (TSh 14,000). Both prices are from Shoppers.
- **Well sourced:** night pads, soap, bags, hot water bottle, the treats and the medicines are Tanzanian listings opened on 10 Nov (Shoppers and AfyaDepot). That is 53% of the monthly shelf value.
- **Not well sourced:** the default day pad (Softcare) is a Tanzanian listing whose host could not be reached. Liners and most other pad brands are Kenyan prices that were not re-checked. Shoppers lists only three pads and no liners, and AfyaDepot lists one pad line. A distributor quote is needed, so the box is **not final**.

## 1. Who it is for, and the guidance it is built from

**Who.** Girls and women in Dar es Salaam who menstruate, from about 12 years to menopause. Teenage girls are served through this pack too (it can ride in the Binti box when that box is built). The need is simple and monthly, and it fails in predictable ways: she runs out mid-period, falls back on cloth or toilet paper, has nowhere safe to throw a used pad, or lives with pain or bleeding that should be seen by a clinic.

**Guidance basis:**

**When to see a clinic** (the `clinic_line`, on the card and in the app):
- a pad soaks through within an hour or two;
- bleeding lasts more than 7 days, or comes with large clots;
- she feels dizzy, breathless or very tired (WHO anaemia signs);
- pain is not eased by tablets and heat, or keeps her from school or work (WHO: severe period pain is a symptom of endometriosis);
- she bleeds between periods or after sex;
- a period is missed and she may be pregnant.
- Fever, a rash or feeling faint while using a cup or tampon means the clinic the same day.

The 1–2 hour and 7-day thresholds are from NHS pages cited in the earlier catalogue and **not re-opened**. A Tanzanian Ministry of Health menstrual-health guideline was not searched for in this round. WHO and UNICEF are the basis.

## 2. What changed from the earlier showroom box (`part3c.js` `D.PKG.siku`, "Period care")

- **Name and scope:** "Period care / Siku zake" becomes **Afya Siku** (B16, D-3). It is one pack for one person.
- **Tiers:** the Essential/Comfort/Luxury brand ladders became one list of choices per item in about the same price range. The tiers differ only by the luxury line (D-4, D4).
- **Removed:**
  - tampons: not in the brief's content list, and they carry the toxic-shock rule. A member can buy them from the shelf.
  - intimate wash and intimate wipes: the guidance is water and mild soap. A pH-balanced wash is noted as a higher-priced swap.
  - ginger tea and the pocket tissues: not in the content list; disposal bags replace the tissue-wrapping role.
  - the chocolate "Zawadi": replaced by the priced tier treats.
  - iron and pain tablets as priced goods: both are now `kind: "medicine"`, partner pharmacy, not priced (B4, D-5). The old box priced ibuprofen and iron inside the box.
- **Changed:**
  - day pads: Softcare stays the default (as in the old Essential tier) and is the Afya Choice; the Comfort and Luxury defaults (Always, Kotex) become swaps.
  - night pads: the default is now the Tanzanian Always Maxi Thick Extra Long from Shoppers.
  - soap: a mild bar replaces the intimate wash.
  - disposal bags: Shoppers sandwich bags replace the Ubbi nappy sacks (TSh 146,000, out of range).
  - hot water bottle: the AfyaDepot bottle with cloth cover replaces Kenyan prices.
- **Prices:** every Kenyan row is recomputed from its KES original at 20.33 (the code used × 20) and marked "not re-checked".
- **Delivery:** the code charged S packing and delivery on top of the goods. Under D4 that cannot be funded (section 5), so the pack rides in another Afya box or is collected in store.

## 5. Price check (D4)

- **Formula.** Shelf value = Σ default-brand price × units ÷ pack units, over goods. Medicines, the optional reusable row and the luxury line are excluded.
- **Member price.** Shelf × (1 − 3% / 6% / 9%), rounded to the nearest 500 and never above shelf.
- **Margin.** Margin = price − goods at landed cost (78% of shelf, R3 planning share, not a supplier quote) − packing − delivery − 1% points.
- **No partner service is included** (no service fee).

- The pack does not pay its way on its own.

- **Why this fix.** It is the only option that keeps a monthly pack timed before each period and passes the D4 check on every plan. It also uses a delivery that is already paid for.
- **Who it fits.** Most members it serves already take another box. Women take Afya Glow, mothers take Mama na Mtoto, and girls will take Binti.
- **Timing.** The member sets her period dates. The app moves the host box's delivery date to a few days before her next period is due, or, where the host box has a fixed date, it ships in the host box before that date.
- **No other Afya box.** She collects the pack in store, the D4 fallback. Her price and margin are the same, and no delivery is offered.
- **Rounding.** On a pack this small, rounding to 500 makes the 6- and 12-month prices equal (7,500, a 7.4% saving). The 12-month plan still gives the first box at 21,000 against 21,500.
- **Two costs are not yet counted.**
  - The pouch that keeps the pack discreet inside the host box. The 12-month margin absorbs up to TSh 1,111 a month.
  - A possible step up in the host box's size (S → M adds 1,500 to that box's packing).
  - Both go to the first packing test.
- **TZ share.** These are the shares of shelf value from Tanzanian listings. "Re-opened" counts only rows opened on 10 Nov. The Softcare day pad (31% of the monthly value) is a Tanzanian listing that could not be re-opened. Liners (16%) are Kenyan.
- **Sensitivity.** If landed cost turns out above about 92% of shelf, the 12-month plan fails even as a rider. Pads and liners are 70% of the monthly value, so a distributor quote on pads decides it.

## 6. JSON notes

- `delivery: "monthly"` (the SCHEMA value). Two fields are added and are not yet in SCHEMA.md:
  - `delivery_mode: "with-another-box"`;
  - `delivery_note` (en/sw).
- `box_size: "S"` is kept for the schema. It is the size used in the standalone comparison; as a rider the pack is a pouch.
- There are two stages: `m1` (first box) and `m` (every month). The hot water bottle ships once, so SCHEMA's rule puts it in the stage where it ships. Each stage carries `pricing` with the D4 numbers. `pricing_rule.standalone` holds table A.
- `optional: true` on `reus` (see section 3). Medicines have `units: 0` (dose set by the pharmacist or clinic), as in Mama Mjamzito.
- `services: []` and `services_note`: no partner service is sensible here. The member's own clinic is the referral.
- Validated with node:
  - every visible string has `en` and `sw`;
  - every item has a numeric `units`, exactly one `default` and one `afya_choice`;
  - every brand has a numeric `pack_units` and price, an https source, and a status; KE rows carry `orig` at 20.33 and the EST row carries its basis;
  - the stored shelf value equals the recomputed one (8,095).
