# R6 — Mama na Mtoto (mother and baby, birth to 36 months)

Feeds section 3.5 (the pilot package page). Prices checked 9–10 Nov 2026. Data file: `data/boxes/mama-na-mtoto.json` (built from this research; `final: false`, reasons below). Pregnancy is the separate box Mama Mjamzito (decision D-12).

## In plain language

- The box serves a mother and child from birth to the third birthday. It is delivered monthly, in nine stage boxes: month 1, a first box and repeat boxes for months 2–6, 7–12, 13–24 and 25–36.
- **Month 1 (TSh 291,077 shelf value):** newborn nappies, cotton wool, wipes, barrier jelly, baby soap, laundry powder and hand soap, plus a thermometer, 5 bodysuits, a hooded towel, a nail clipper, a treated net, maternity pads, breast pads, the mother's repellent and a first toy.
- **Months 2–6:** the same everyday goods with medium nappies. The first box of this stage adds a manual breast pump and milk bags for the return to work.
- **Months 7–12:** first-foods kit and teether, water treatment, toothpaste and brush, and sanitary pads for the mother.
- **Months 13–24:** large nappies, a net for the child's own bed and plasters. **Months 25–36:** training pants, a potty and a replacement family net.
- Every stage has one cheap developmental toy a month, from a list of 4–5 options.
- Iron + folic acid, ORS and zinc are listed but not sold by Afya: the partner pharmacy sells them (B4).
- **Member price, recurring months:** TSh 96,500–127,500 a month on the 12-month to 1-month plans. **Over the 36 months:** an average of TSh 115,972 a month (1-month plan), 112,486 (6-month) and 108,833 (12-month), against a shelf value of 119,658. Packing and delivery are included (D4).
- **Taste of luxury** (priced as its own line): Premium adds Dove Shower Cream 500 ml (TSh 12,000 a month). Luxury adds a Bath & Body Works shower gel (TSh 32,528 a month, Kenyan price).
- **Well sourced:** of 104 brand rows, 41 were re-checked on Tanzanian sites on 9–10 Nov (21 AfyaDepot, 20 Shoppers). These cover the default nappies in every stage except months 13–24, plus wipes, laundry, barrier jelly, baby soap, repellent and most pad choices. On the default brands, 74–96% of each stage's value is from Tanzanian listings.
- **Not well sourced:** 41 rows are Kenyan prices and 20 come from Tanzanian sites this session cannot reach; none of these 61 was re-opened. There is no shelf price for the treated net (estimate, TSh 10,000), so the box is **not final**. Several items have fewer than 4 brands.

## 1. Who it is for, and the guidance it is built from

**Who.** Mothers in Dar es Salaam with a baby from birth to 36 months. The box covers the baby's daily goods and the mother's own recovery and breastfeeding needs. The 0–6-week window carries the highest risk: about 24 of every 1,000 Tanzanian babies die in the first month, and only about half of mothers and newborns are checked within two days of birth (TDHS-MIS 2022, cited in the pilot code). The box therefore puts most of its weight on the first month, where the partner services also sit: the midwife home visit and the postnatal check.

**Guidance basis** (the full list with links is in section 8):

| Topic | What the guidance says | How the box applies it | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|

| Breastfeeding | WHO + UNICEF: start within 1 hour, exclusive for 6 months, continue to 2 years or beyond with safe complementary foods from 6 months | No formula or bottles in the box; breast pads (months 1–6), pump and milk bags (month 2) | WHO fact sheet opened 10 Nov |
| Complementary feeding | WHO 2023 guideline for 6–23 months | First-foods kit at month 7; food itself is on the customer-chosen nutrition shelf (R9) | WHO page opened 10 Nov |

**Clinic line (B16, in the JSON):** "Which medicines the mother or child take, at what dose, how often the baby is weighed and checked, and when to see a doctor are decided by your clinic. The box never replaces clinic visits or vaccinations; a fever in a baby under three months, or any danger sign, goes to the clinic the same day."

## 2. What changed from the pilot code (`part3c.js` `D.PKG.mama`)

- **Structure.** Stages a–e are kept, but each stage with durable items now has a **first box** (b1, c1, d1, e1). This follows SCHEMA.md: an item bought once is listed in the stage where it ships, not spread over every month.
- **Tiers.** All tiers share the same core. Premium and Luxury differ only by one "taste of luxury" line, priced outside the saving (D4). The pilot's Essential/Comfort/Luxury brand ladders became one list of choices per item, in roughly the same price range. Higher-range swaps are noted on the row.
- **Removed as filler or against guidance:**
  - the monthly gift (Zawadi), which is replaced by the tier luxury line;
  - the mother's body wash, body lotion and lip balm, which had no clinical reason;
  - intimate wash: the NHS advice is water and plain soap, so a dedicated wash is not needed;
  - disposal bags (Ubbi at TSh 146,000 for 400, out of range);
  - muslin burp cloths;
  - brush and comb;
  - cotton wool after month 1 (wipes take over);
  - barrier cream in stage e (pull-up pants).
  - The Msingi-only items (cloth nappies, laundry bar, porridge flour) belong to the parked Msingi edition and to the R9 nutrition shelf.
- **Added or split:**
  - nail clipper and towel split out of the grooming set;
  - toothpaste and toothbrush split;
  - zinc split from ORS;
  - nappy size S kept for month 2 (babies are around 5 kg).
- **Prices.**
  - Every Kenyan row is recomputed from its KES original at 20.33. The code used ×20.
  - AfyaDepot and Shoppers prices replace code prices wherever the same product was found: Softcare S/M, Sleepy, Huggies pants, wipes, Zincast, Sudocrem, the Shoppers soaps, laundry, pads and repellents.
- **Afya Choice** is new: one value brand per item. The default brand used for the headline price is the Afya Choice for every item. The Afya Choice is the lowest-cost suitable row with a real listing: not the estimated cards, and not a general (non-baby) nail clipper.

## 3. Stage-by-stage items

Each row lists the brand choices. **Bold** = default brand used for the headline price; ★ = Afya Choice (value brand). Monthly cost = price × units ÷ pack units. Status: TZ = Tanzanian listing; KE = Kenyan listing converted at KES 20.33 (BoT mean, 10 Nov 2026); EST = labelled estimate. Medicines are listed but not priced in the box (B4).

How stages work: a stage with durable items has a **first box** (b1, c1, d1, e1) that ships them once; the following boxes (b, c, d, e) carry only the monthly goods. Consumables used more slowly than one pack a month (toothpaste, toothbrush, water treatment, barrier cream, laundry) are charged for one month’s use and a new pack ships when the last runs low.

## 6. Price check (D4)

- **Formula.** Shelf value = Σ default-brand price × units ÷ pack units, over goods and toys. Medicines and the luxury line are excluded.
- **Member price.** Shelf × (1 − 3% / 6% / 9%), rounded to the nearest 500 and never above shelf. Packing (L 6,500 · M 5,000) and delivery (3,500) are inside this price.
- **Service fees** (partner price × 1.25, charged in the month of the service) are added separately once R7 sets them.

- **Margin check** (D4 "Check"). The check uses a flat 78% landed-cost share for all goods, a planning assumption from R3 and not a supplier quote, plus packing, delivery and 1% points.
  - Every stage and plan is ≥ 0.
  - The 12-month plan ranges from TSh 2,651 (months 14–24) to 25,310 (month 1).
  - Months 14–24 is the thinnest stage. If landed cost turns out above 80%, the 12-month plan in that stage would fail; R3's rule then calls for less frequent delivery or collection in store.
- **How the numbers move.** Nappies are 33–67% of each stage's value (33% in month 1, 56–67% from month 13). In months 26–36 the AfyaDepot Softcare pants price (600 a pant) is used. The 2 Nov superstore price (380) would lower stage e's shelf value by about TSh 26,000.
