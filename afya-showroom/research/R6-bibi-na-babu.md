Feeds section 3.5. Prices checked 10 Nov 2026. Data file: `data/boxes/bibi-na-babu.json` (validated against `data/boxes/SCHEMA.md`). Built from the earlier code box `D.PKG.senior` in `part3c.js`. The showroom code and the brief are unchanged.

## In plain language

- Bibi na Babu is for a person aged 60 and over living at home, and for the family member who cares for them. It is built on WHO’s ageing, falls and oral-health fact sheets and the WHO ICOPE guidelines.
- It comes at three levels, which the family can change at any renewal: **Active** (independent), **Assisted** (needs some help, light incontinence) and **Bed-bound** (full care in bed). Each level opens with a first box carrying the durable items once: blood-pressure monitor, thermometer, pill organiser, nail clipper, treated net, and for the first two levels a non-slip bath mat and night light (plus a resistance band at Active, a walking stick at Assisted).
- Everyday goods: body lotion, mild soap, petroleum jelly, sensitive-teeth toothpaste and a soft brush at every level. Assisted adds night briefs, wipes, barrier, tie bags, gloves and hand rub for the carer; Bed-bound adds three briefs a day and underpads.
- **Member price, 12-month plan:** Active TSh 314,500 for the first 3-month box, then 86,000 every 3 months; Assisted 318,000 then 86,500 a month; Bed-bound 393,000 then 209,000 a month. Over a year that is about **47,708 / 105,792 / 224,333 a month**. Packing and delivery are included (D4).
- **Fix applied:** delivered monthly, the Active level’s few toiletries (about TSh 31,500 a month) lose about 3,200 a month. It is therefore delivered **once every 3 months** with 3 months of goods; then every plan pays its way. Assisted and Bed-bound pay their way monthly.
- No medicines are in the box. The clinic decides tests and medicines; the partner pharmacy supplies them (B4, B16 clinic line below).
- **Taste of luxury** (its own line): Premium adds CeraVe hand cream (TSh 25,500, TZ); Luxury adds Bath & Body Works Ultra Shea body cream (TSh 50,825, Kenyan price).
- **Sourcing:** 47 of 83 brand rows are Tanzanian listings opened today; 34 are Kenyan prices from the code; 2 are estimates (the treated net, a band set). On the default brands, 64–92% of each stage’s value is Tanzanian. Every core item has a sourced price or, for the net, a labelled estimate where no listing exists, so `final` is true.

## 1. Who it is for, and the guidance it is built from

**English.** For a person aged 60 and over living at home in Dar es Salaam, and for the family member who cares for them. Tanzania counted about 3.5 million people aged 60 and over in the 2022 census, and WHO expects one person in six worldwide to be over 60 by 2030. Most older Tanzanians are cared for at home by their families, often by a daughter or grandchild who also works.

The box is built on WHO’s view of healthy ageing: keep the person’s own capacity for as long as possible and adapt the home around them. It follows three WHO fact sheets and one WHO guideline: Ageing and health (falls, urinary incontinence and pressure ulcers are the geriatric syndromes that follow loss of independence), Falls (71% of deaths from falls are among people aged 60 and over; exercise that challenges balance and targeted home-hazard reduction work), Oral health (fluoride protects teeth at every age), and the ICOPE guidelines on integrated care for older people (mobility, nutrition, sight, hearing, continence, mood). Blood pressure is in the box because only 17.6% of Tanzanians with high blood pressure know they have it (WHO country profile, 2012).

The box comes at three levels, chosen with the family and changed as needs change: Active (independent), Assisted (needs some help, light incontinence) and Bed-bound (full care in bed). Each level opens with a first box that brings the durable items once; after that only the everyday goods arrive. The Active level arrives once every three months, because a monthly box of a few toiletries cannot pay its own delivery.

**Kiswahili.** Kwa mtu mwenye umri wa miaka 60 na zaidi anayeishi nyumbani Dar es Salaam, na kwa mwanafamilia anayemhudumia. Sensa ya 2022 ilihesabu takriban watu milioni 3.5 wenye umri wa miaka 60 na zaidi nchini Tanzania, na WHO inatarajia mtu mmoja kati ya sita duniani kuwa na zaidi ya miaka 60 ifikapo 2030. Wazee wengi wa Kitanzania huhudumiwa nyumbani na familia zao, mara nyingi na binti au mjukuu ambaye pia anafanya kazi.

Sanduku limejengwa juu ya mtazamo wa WHO wa kuzeeka kwa afya: kulinda uwezo wa mtu mwenyewe kwa muda mrefu iwezekanavyo na kurekebisha nyumba kumzunguka. Linafuata taarifa tatu za WHO na mwongozo mmoja wa WHO: Kuzeeka na afya (kuanguka, kushindwa kuzuia mkojo na vidonda vya kulala ni matatizo makuu yanayofuata kupoteza uwezo wa kujitegemea), Kuanguka (71% ya vifo vinavyotokana na kuanguka ni vya watu wenye miaka 60 na zaidi; mazoezi ya mizani na kupunguza hatari nyumbani hufanya kazi), Afya ya kinywa (floraidi hulinda meno katika umri wowote), na miongozo ya ICOPE ya huduma jumuishi kwa wazee (kutembea, lishe, kuona, kusikia, kuzuia mkojo, hali ya moyo). Kipimo cha shinikizo la damu kimo kwa sababu ni 17.6% tu ya Watanzania wenye shinikizo la juu la damu wanaojua hali yao (taarifa ya WHO ya nchi, 2012).

Sanduku lina viwango vitatu, vinavyochaguliwa pamoja na familia na kubadilishwa mahitaji yanapobadilika: Anajitegemea, Anasaidiwa (anahitaji msaada kiasi, kuvuja kidogo kwa mkojo) na Yuko kitandani (huduma kamili kitandani). Kila kiwango huanza na sanduku la kwanza linaloleta vifaa vya kudumu mara moja; baada ya hapo huja bidhaa za kila siku tu. Kiwango cha Anajitegemea huletwa mara moja kila miezi mitatu, kwa sababu sanduku la kila mwezi la vitu vichache vya usafi haliwezi kulipia usafirishaji wake.

### Clinic line (B16)

- **EN:** Your clinic decides your blood-pressure and sugar tests and how often to take them, any medicine and its dose, and whether incontinence, a sore or a fall needs treatment. Afya does not sell medicines: the licensed partner pharmacy supplies them on the clinic’s prescription. A sudden change in alertness, a fall with pain, a fever, or a red or open sore goes to the clinic the same day; do not wait for the next box.
- **SW:** Kliniki yako ndiyo inayoamua vipimo vya shinikizo la damu na sukari na mara ngapi vichukuliwe, dawa yoyote na dozi yake, na kama kushindwa kuzuia mkojo, kidonda au kuanguka kunahitaji tiba. Afya haiuzi dawa: famasia mshirika yenye leseni huzitoa kwa maagizo ya kliniki. Mabadiliko ya ghafla ya fahamu, kuanguka na kuumia, homa, au kidonda chekundu au kilicho wazi hupelekwa kliniki siku hiyo hiyo; usisubiri sanduku lijalo.

## 7. Does it pay its way?

**Yes, on every plan and at every level, after one fix.**
- **The fix: the Active level is delivered once every 3 months.** As a monthly S box, Active’s goods (about TSh 31,500 a month) would give a 12-month margin of about −3,180 a month (−9,540 over the quarter, last column above). As one M box every 3 months the margin is +2,960 a box. This is the R3 rule: less frequent delivery when a box is too small.
- **Thin points.** Active repeat boxes and Assisted monthly boxes are thin on the 12-month plan (3.4% and 8.8%). They break even if goods land at about 81% of shelf on average; the R3 planning shares give 78% and 73%. Brief supplier quotes (Harmony, Finecare) decide the Assisted box.
- First boxes carry large margins (about 64,000–74,000 on 12 months) because devices are classed at the 72% and 50% landed shares; these are planning assumptions, not quotes.
