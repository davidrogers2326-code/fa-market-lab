# R9 — Lishe: the customer-chosen nutrition shelf for Mama na Mtoto

Feeds sections 3.5 and 3.6. Prices checked 10 Nov 2026. Data file: `data/nutrition.json` (27 rows). Draft for the founder; the showroom code and the brief are unchanged.

## In plain language

- Lishe is a shelf beside the Mama na Mtoto and Mama Mjamzito boxes. **The customer chooses what to add. Nothing on it is put in a box for her.**
- For the first 6 months a baby needs breast milk only (WHO and UNICEF). Nothing on the shelf is labelled or described for a baby under 6 months.
- From 6 months the shelf offers complementary foods, eaten alongside breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond: uji flours and finger millet, infant cereals, groundnut paste, pulses and oil to enrich porridge.
- Cow's milk is listed as a drink from 12 months. WHO's 2023 guideline says animal milk, not follow-up formula, for children 12–23 months who drink milk other than breast milk.
- **Infant formula is not on the shelf and never in a box.** It is never discounted, never earns points and never appears in pictures or offers. A family whose health worker advises it can ask at the counter or the partner pharmacy, at the ordinary price. This follows the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes and Tanzania's 2013 regulations.
- Follow-on formula and growing-up milks are not stocked at all.
- 19 of 27 rows are Tanzanian prices opened today on Shoppers Supermarket. The 8 uji and porridge-flour rows have only Kenyan prices: neither Shoppers' online shop nor AfyaDepot lists uji flour. Finding Tanzanian uji wa lishe brands and prices is the main open item.
- Prices run from TSh 1,700 (half-litre of milk) to 26,200 (Cerelac 350 g). A 1 kg uji flour costs about 3,200–4,500, against 25,500 for 350 g of infant cereal: 16–23 times cheaper per gram.

## 1. What is appropriate by age (WHO and UNICEF)

| Age | Guidance | What Afya sells for this age |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 months | Breastfeeding starts within 1 hour of birth, is **exclusive for the first 6 months**, and continues to 2 years or beyond (WHO fact sheet, *Infant and young child feeding*; opened 10 Nov 2026) | **Nothing.** No food, water, tea or cereal is sold or described for this age |
| 6–8 months | Complementary foods start at 6 months, alongside breast milk. Begin with purées, mashes and semi-solid foods, **2–3 times a day** (WHO health topic, *Complementary feeding*) | Uji flours and finger millet, infant cereals, mashed pulses, smooth groundnut paste thinned into porridge, a little oil |
| 9–11 months | **3–4 meals a day**; finger foods by about 8 months | The same, thicker; family foods mashed |
| 12–23 months | 3–4 meals plus 1–2 snacks; by 12 months most children eat family foods, including animal-source foods. Children fed milks other than breast milk: animal milk; **follow-up formulas are not recommended** (WHO 2023 complementary-feeding guideline, *conditional recommendation, low-certainty evidence*) | Full-cream milk (fresh, UHT, family powder) as a drink, oats, family flours |
| All ages | Avoid choking forms (e.g. whole grapes, raw carrots); no tea, coffee or sugary drinks; limit juice (WHO health topic) | No whole nuts for young children; nuts only ground. No sweetened drinks on the shelf |
| Pregnancy and breastfeeding | Healthy eating and staying active (WHO ANC 2016, A.1.1); fibre for constipation (D.5); no routine multiple-micronutrient supplements (A.6) | Oats, finger-millet flour, unsalted cashews. Supplements are sold by the partner pharmacy, not on this shelf (B4) |

The WHO 2023 milk wording is quoted from summaries of the guideline (ENN *Field Exchange* 72; Baby Milk Action). The guideline's own PDF sits on iris.who.int, which this session's proxy refused. The WHO publication page itself was opened: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240081864.

For non-breastfed infants of 6–11 months, the same guideline allows either milk formula or full-fat animal milk. European paediatric societies (ESPGHAN and others, 2024) dispute the animal-milk advice for high-income settings. **Afya therefore lists milk as a drink from 12 months only, and as an ingredient in cooked porridge from 6 months, and leaves the choice of milk for a non-breastfed infant under 12 months to the health worker.**

## 2. Marketing of breast-milk substitutes: the rules Afya follows

**International Code (WHA 1981) and later resolutions.** WHO's summary (eLENA, opened 10 Nov 2026): "Breast-milk substitutes should be available when needed, but not be promoted."

Article 5 of the Code prohibits the following to the public:
- advertising;
- free samples;
- point-of-sale promotion, such as special displays, discount coupons, premiums, special sales and tie-in sales;
- gifts.

*The Code PDF itself, on apps.who.int, was refused here. The Article 5 list is taken from the code's existing reference (`who_code`).*

WHO's 2016 *Guidance on ending the inappropriate promotion of foods for infants and young children* (WHA 69.9) covers foods for children under 36 months. It comes with an information note classifying follow-up formulas for 6–36 months as breast-milk substitutes (WHO page, opened 10 Nov 2026).

**Tanzania.** The *Tanzania Food, Drugs and Cosmetics (Marketing of Foods and Designated Products for Infants and Young Children) Regulations, 2013* are Government Notice No. 60 of 15 March 2013, made under the Food, Drugs and Cosmetics Act (Cap. 219). They cover the promotion of breast-milk substitutes, complementary foods and designated products. *Snippet only:* the text was found through search but not opened; tradebarriers.org, tanzlii.org and faolex.fao.org were refused. The code's reference (Pries et al. 2016) says the Regulations cover products for children up to 5 years. The current text and any amendments must be confirmed with TMDA (the former TFDA) before launch.

## 4. How the shelf works

- **Customer-chosen, never assigned.** The member adds Lishe items to a box delivery or buys them in store. The box engine never adds them by default and never makes them a condition of a plan.
- **Lishe items take the ordinary member saving** like other goods; they are complementary foods and family foods, not designated products. Founder to confirm this once the Tanzanian Regulations are read (Open item 1), since they may also restrict the promotion of complementary foods.
- **No baby faces, no idealising images** (B12 already bans faces). Every infant-food row states its age band and that breastfeeding continues.
- **Afya own-label.** The range catalogue (`range/lib.js`, `mb_nutr`) shows "Afya own-label" infant cereals, weaning mixes and uji at prices below the brands (status "OWN"). Those are not prices, and an own-label infant food needs TBS/TMDA registration and labelling under the 2013 Regulations. Own-label registration is launch Phase 2 (D-14). They are left out of `nutrition.json`.

## 5. Open items

1. **Read the 2013 Regulations** (GN 60/2013) and any amendments with TMDA. Confirm:
   - whether complementary foods for under-24s may take a plan discount or points;
   - whether stage-1 formula may be sold on request in store;
   - what labelling the shelf must show. The hosts were refused here.
2. **Founder decision:** stage-1 infant formula on request only, behind the counter or through the partner pharmacy (recommended), or not sold at all.
3. **Tanzanian uji wa lishe brands and prices.** None are online on Shoppers or AfyaDepot. Collect them at Shoppers' branches, the Kariakoo calls or from distributors. Local fortified blends are to be identified on the shelf, not from memory.
4. **Range catalogue** (`range/lib.js`, `mb_nutr`, 73 products) lists, with prices and a "WHO Code: never promoted" chip:
   - 6 stage-1 formulas, 3 follow-on, 3 growing-up and 2 special formulas;
   - plus "2 ×" multi-pack rows for cereals and flours.

   Showing formula as priced catalogue cards, and multi-buy packs of infant cereal, may itself count as promotion. Recommend removing the formula families from the showroom catalogue and keeping one text line instead. This is a code change for a later phase, not done here.
5. **Added sugar:** read the Cerelac labels on the shelf. WHO advises against foods high in sugar for young children; the code cites a 2025 report on added sugar in Cerelac (not re-opened).
6. **Peanut butter:** confirm a TBS mark on each brand (aflatoxin; code reference `afla_pb`, not re-opened). Smigi beans: pack size not stated on the listing.
7. **Kiswahili for native review:**
   - *vyakula vya nyongeza* (complementary foods);
   - *uji wa lishe*;
   - *maziwa ya kopo ya hatua inayofuata* (follow-up formula);
   - *maziwa mbadala ya maziwa ya mama* (breast-milk substitutes);
   - *bidhaa maalumu* (designated products);
   - *sumu kuvu (aflatoxin)*;
   - *urutubishaji* (fortification).
