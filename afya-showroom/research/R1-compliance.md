# R1 — Regulation and compliance (Afya Basics)

Research package R1 from the brief (Part F). It feeds showroom sections 4.5, 7.1, 4.2 and 3.6.
**All pages were opened on 9 Nov 2026** unless a row says otherwise. Every TSh figure converted from USD uses the Bank of Tanzania mean rate of **TSh 2,642.50 per US$ (trading date 10 Nov 2026)**, from `research/fx.md` (https://www.bot.go.tz/ExchangeRate/excRates).

---

## In plain language

1. You need four kinds of permission before opening: **a company** (BRELA), **a tax number** (TRA), **two business licences** (one from BRELA because Afya imports, one from the city council for the shop), and **product-safety registrations** (TBS for cosmetics and food, TMDA for the shop's medical devices).
2. The fixed government fees to open are small: about **TSh 1.04 million** in total (company, two licences, three premises registrations). Every one of these is taken from an official fee page.
3. The big cost we did not know about is **TBS product registration: TSh 440,000 for every cosmetic product Afya imports itself** (half price for a colour or scent variant). Infant formula is TSh 1,000,000 per product and food supplements TSh 800,000. Registrations last 5 years.
4. With 20 self-imported cosmetic products, the compliance budget is about **TSh 11.1 million**. That fits the brief's TSh 8–12M placeholder. If Afya buys already-registered products from local distributors, it falls to about **TSh 2.3 million**.
5. Confirmed: cosmetics and food moved from TMDA to TBS on 1 July 2019. GN 681 of 2021 is the rule. The pre-shipment certificate (PVoC) costs **0.5% of the factory price, at least US$250 and at most US$5,000** per shipment.
6. Changed from what we believed: Bureau Veritas is **not** a PVoC contractor; for China it is CCIC, Intertek, SGS, TÜV Rheinland or WSCT. The "TSh 50,000 importer registration" does **not** appear in the TBS fee regulations. The TMDA shop permit is **TSh 100,000**, not 60,000.
7. Port taxes went up on 1 July 2026. The customs processing fee is now **1% of FOB** (it was 0.6%). Excise on skin, hair, perfume and deodorant products rose from **10% to 15%**. Import duty on these goods is 35% and VAT is 18%.
8. The Afya Check kiosk **is a medical device** under TMDA rules. Blood pressure, temperature and oxygen readings "in routine checkups" are Class B, and there is no "wellness" exemption. Buy it from a TMDA-registered local supplier that holds the registration; registering it ourselves costs US$2,500 (TSh 6.6M).
9. Nappies, sanitary pads and adult incontinence pads sit on a TMDA "may be a medical device" list. They also have compulsory TBS standards, so they need the PVoC certificate either way.
10. Still needs a phone call: a **written clearing-agent quote** for a 20 ft container; whether TBS charges an importer registration fee separate from the TSh 440,000 product fee; and TMDA's written view on the kiosk and on nappies and pads.


> Reviewer note (9 Nov 2026): the TBS PVoC guide's fee table gives three routes: Route A 0.50% of FOB, Route B 0.45%, Route C 0.25%, each with a US$250 minimum and US$5,000 maximum (`pvoc_guide`, TBS). Registering frequently shipped products under Route B therefore also lowers the per-shipment fee.

## 5. TMDA questions

### 5.1 Retail premises for BP monitors, glucometers, thermometers
- **A TMDA-registered premises is required.** Cap 219 s.18(1): "A person shall not manufacture for sale, sell, supply or store products regulated under this Act except in premises registered under this section for that purpose." TMDA's fee regulations name "Retail Medical Device Outlets TZS 100,000". Source: https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/1779257152-2025%20TMDA%20ACT.pdf (Cap 219 R.E. 2023); 2024 fee amendment.
- **Documents.** TMDA lists: "Copy of Certificate of Registration from … BRELA; Tax Payer Identification Number (TIN); A copy of a contract with superintendent/in charge of the business; A copy of registration certificate of the superintendent issued by the respective councils or professional boards; Premises layout/sketch; Copy of Memorandum and articles of Association." Applications go through the online Trader Portal at the zone office (Dar es Salaam = Eastern Zone). "The business permit issued shall expire on 30th June every year." Sources: https://www.tmda.go.tz/pages/registration-and-licensing-of-premises-1 ; https://www.tmda.go.tz/pages/registration-and-licensing-of-premises.
- **Responsible person.** The GSDP Regulations 2021 (TMDA labels its copy "Draft") reg. 16(1): "The owner of premises shall appoint a person as responsible person whom shall meet the qualifications as specified in the Pharmacy Act, Veterinary Act, Health Laboratory Practitioners Act or any other relevant laws." Afya therefore needs a qualified, council-registered in-charge for the device shelf. **Which cadre is acceptable for a retail device outlet is not stated: ask TMDA.**
- **Importing devices directly is restricted.** TMDA's import guideline (Rev 02, May 2024) s.1.1.1 lists who may import: "Government and Non-Governmental Institutions; Medical devices and In vitro diagnostics wholesalers; … Manufacturers; Clinical Trial Sponsor …; Recipients of Donations". A **retail** outlet is not on the list. The guideline also states: "All medical devices … to be imported must have marketing authorization (registration or notification) by TMDA" (1.2.1). The permit must be applied for "At least 6 weeks prior to shipment" and is processed "within 7 days". Shelf life on arrival must be at least 60% (shelf life over 24 months) or 80% (24 months or less). Sources: https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/publications/en1755006678-en1728063896-GUIDELINES%20FOR%20IMPORTATION%20AND%20EXPORTATION%20OF%20MEDICAL%20DEVICES%20-%2017.05%20(1).pdf ; https://www.tmda.go.tz/pages/import-and-export-control-of-medical-devices.
  **Implication:** at launch, buy devices from TMDA-registered local wholesalers. To import devices itself, Afya must also register as a wholesale or importing premises (TSh 300,000) and use only registered or notified device models.

### 5.2 Which device classes need registration or notification
- **Notification** "is the marketing authorization route that is reserved for class A medical devices and in-vitro diagnostic devices that do not meet the criteria for registration process". Registration criteria are "a) Active devices; b) Measuring function; and c) Sterile". Class A devices with those features "may be notified provided that they are not used directly in diagnosis and/ or they are designed for home use only. Example: body scales for monitoring weight, equipment for height measurement".
- **Validity:** registration lasts 5 years and notification 3 years.
- **The marketing-authorisation holder** must be "a resident of Tanzania and must be licensed by TMDA as a medical device dealer". A foreign maker appoints a "Local Responsible Person".
- Source: TMDA Compendium, June 2024, PDF pp. 16, 18, 22, 58 (URL above).

| Device | Class (TMDA rule) | Route |
|---|---|---|
| BP monitor, digital thermometer, pulse oximeter (home or routine use) | **Class B**: "devices intended to be used to obtain readings of vital physiological signals in routine checkups and in self-monitoring are in Class B" (Rule 10(i)(c)) | Registration (imported US$2,500 = TSh 6,606,250) |
| Glucose meter + strips (self-test) | **Class C** IVD: "IVDDs intended for self-testing are classified as Class C"; example "Blood glucose monitoring" | Registration (IVD Class C US$1,500 = TSh 3,963,750) |
| Pregnancy / ovulation self-test | Class B IVD: "Example for self-testing class B: Pregnancy self test, Fertility testing" | Registration (US$1,000 = TSh 2,642,500) |
| Bathroom scale, height rod (home or monitoring) | Class A, notifiable example | Notification (US$50 = TSh 132,125) |

These are fees paid by the **registration holder**, normally the brand's local distributor, not by a retailer. Afya pays them only if it chooses to be the holder.

### 5.3 The Afya Check kiosk (height, weight, BMI, BP, SpO2, temperature, body composition)
- **It is a medical device, and the class is B or higher.** The kiosk measures blood pressure, temperature and oxygen saturation. TMDA's Rule 10(i)(c) puts devices "intended to allow direct … monitoring of vital physiological processes" in Class B, giving "electronic thermometers" and "blood pressure monitors" as examples. Vital signs include "blood pressure and body temperature", and "routine checkups" fall in Class B. TMDA also says "Blood pressure monitors, even if intended to be used in a gym" are medical devices. Where several rules apply, "the rules resulting in the highest risk classification shall be applicable to the device".
- **No "wellness" or "non-diagnostic" exemption was found** in the compendium. Classification turns on the manufacturer's intended purpose: "based on the stated intended purpose of the product and its mode of action". (Compendium PDF pp. 17, 35–37, 86.)
- **Consequence.** The kiosk model must hold a TMDA marketing authorisation, or be imported under a special permit, before it can be imported. Import guideline 1.2.1 says all imported devices "must have marketing authorization … unless given special approval by the Authority". Special permits for unregistered devices require ISO 13485, a CoC or DoC, approval in an IMDRF founding country or WHO prequalification, and that "The device under request is not available from local suppliers" (1.5.1).
- **Options:**
  - (a) buy the kiosk from a Tanzanian TMDA-licensed dealer who registers it (Class B, US$2,500 = TSh 6,606,250 plus US$200 = TSh 528,500 a year);
  - (b) ask TMDA in writing for a classification opinion before ordering.

  Calling it a "wellness station" does not change the TMDA class.

### 5.4 TMDA fees: 60,000 vs 100,000
Settled above: **TSh 100,000** per year for a retail medical device outlet. Sources: GN 686 of 2021, item 98; 2024 amendment, item 92; TMDA premises fee page.
